§1 Overview

DMS (internally also called EDAMS in some backend identifiers — this is deliberate, not a typo) is Example Corp's document and records management system: upload, review, approve, sign, retain, and eventually dispose of documents, letters, and filled forms under one audit trail.

Every document moves through a small set of states as it's worked on:

Draft → In Review → Active → Archived

A document in Draft is only visible to its owner and admins. Submitting it for approval moves it to In Review, where an approval chain of one or more steps decides its fate — approved steps advance the chain; a decline or a request for changes sends it back to Draft. Once every step approves, it becomes Active (published) and everyone with read access can see it. From there it can be archived, put on legal hold, checked out for a new version, or eventually staged for retention disposal.

The same organization is multi-tenant: your account can belong to more than one tenant (for example "Example Corp" and "Demo Org"), and everything you see — documents, folders, users — is scoped to whichever tenant you're currently working in.

§2 Getting started

Signing in

  1. Open the DMS sign-in page. In production this hands off to Example Corp's shared identity provider (SSO); pick Sign in with Example Corp SSO if that's how your account is provisioned.
  2. Enter your email and password (or select your account from the demo list, in a development environment) and choose Sign in.
  3. If your account has two-factor authentication enabled, you'll be asked for a 6-digit code from your authenticator app before the session completes.

Choosing a tenant

If your account belongs to more than one tenant, you'll land on a tenant picker after signing in — pick the organization you want to work in. You can switch tenants later from the switcher at the top of the sidebar without signing out.

i

Everything you do — uploads, approvals, searches — is scoped to your current tenant. A document that exists in "Demo Org" won't appear while you're working in "Example Corp," even if you technically have access to both.

§3 Roles & permissions

DMS ships with seven roles. What you see in the sidebar — and which buttons are active rather than hidden — is driven entirely by your role's permissions, not by guesswork in the UI.

System Admin

Full access, every permission

  • Everything below the line, plus the Operations console in full

Records Officer

Records & retention specialist

  • Upload, archive, purge documents
  • Retention & legal hold management
  • Approval admin (reassign/cancel chains)

Dept Manager

Runs a department

  • Manage their department & its users/roles
  • Approve documents and letters
  • Approval admin within their scope

Approver

Workflow approvals only

  • Read documents, decide approval steps
  • Review/approve letters
  • No upload, no admin actions

Employee

Read & upload

  • Upload, check out, submit for approval
  • Create letters and fill forms
  • No department or user administration

Auditor

Audit log access

  • Read every document tenant-wide (read-only)
  • Audit trail, retention & legal-hold visibility
  • Decide audit-stage reviews

Chief Executive

Correspondence oversight

  • Read-only across all letters, tenant-wide
  • No document module access by default
  • Standing recipient for oversight notifications
i

A role gives you a bundle of permissions; an admin can additionally grant or restrict access to a specific folder or department through an ACL entry, and legal hold or retention rules can block an action regardless of your role. If a button is greyed out that you expect to use, one of these three layers is usually why — see Troubleshooting.

§4 Documents

The document is the core object in DMS — everything else (approvals, signatures, retention, audit) attaches to one.

Uploading a document

  1. Choose Upload in the sidebar.
  2. Drop a file onto the upload zone, or click it to browse. One file at a time when you also want to set a title; multiple files skip straight to bulk metadata.
  3. Fill in the required fields: Title, Department, Document Type, and a retention trigger date (the date retention counting starts from — often the document's own date, sometimes contract end date, depending on type).
  4. Optionally add a description, tags, a reference number, and a geographic/organizational scope.
  5. Review the summary step and confirm. The document is created in Draft, owned by you.
i

Filenames are sanitized when stored — special characters are stripped from the internal storage key — but the name you see everywhere in the UI is always your original filename. Uploading Q3 Report (final) [v2].pdf works exactly as expected; nothing is silently rejected.

Check-out & versions

Only one person can hold the checkout on a document at a time. While checked out, everyone else can still read the current published version, but can't check it out themselves or start a new one. Uploading through New Version asks for a brief change summary and whether the change is major or minor; the new file becomes the current version and, for approval-required types, may drop the document back to Draft for re-approval if it was already Active.

The Versions tab lists every version with its number, uploader, and date, and lets an authorized user roll a document back to an earlier version if needed.

§5 Approvals

DMS documents, filled forms, and form templates all share one approval engine — a chain of one or more steps, each either a plain review or a final approve, each assigned to a role or to a specific named person.

Submitting for approval

  1. Open the document and go to its Workflow tab.
  2. Choose Submit for approval. If the document type has a standard chain template, its steps pre-fill; any locked step (marked with a lock icon) is mandatory and can't be removed or reordered.
  3. For each unlocked step, pick a step type (Review or Approve), then either a role (e.g. "Dept Manager") or a named person from the picker. The last step in the chain must always be an Approve.
  4. Add more steps with Add step if the type calls for more than one reviewer, then choose Submit.

The document moves to In Review and the first step's assignee is notified immediately.

Deciding a step

If a step is assigned to your role or to you by name, its card on the Workflow tab shows Approve, Request changes, and Decline:

  • Approve — advances to the next step, or, if it was the last one, publishes the document. An optional comment; on the final step you may also be asked to pick an auditor if the document type requires a post-approval audit pass.
  • Request changes — sends the document back to Draft for the author to revise and resubmit. A comment is optional but recommended.
  • Decline — a hard rejection. Requires a written reason.

Reassign & cancel (admin recovery)

Anyone holding approval-admin rights (System Admin, Records Officer, Dept Manager) sees two extra controls on a running chain:

  • Reassign — on a pending step that isn't assigned to you, moves it to a different capable user. Requires a short reason.
  • Cancel chain — stops the whole chain and returns the document to Draft. Requires a reason of at least 10 characters; this can't be undone.
✓

Verified this session: both open as proper in-app dialogs (title, description, Escape-to-close) rather than the browser's own prompt/confirm boxes — reassigning moves the step and notifies the new assignee; cancelling reverts the document and notifies the original submitter with the reason given.

§6 My Work

One inbox for everything currently waiting on you: approval steps (documents, forms, and letters), signature requests, audit-stage reviews, and incoming correspondence assigned to you.

Each card links straight to where you act on it — an approval step opens the document's Workflow tab, a letter opens the letter, a signature request opens the signing page. The four counters at the top (Approvals, Signatures, Audits, Assigned correspondence) update the moment something new arrives — you don't need to refresh the page.

✓

Verified & fixed this session: My Work now updates live when a new approval step, signature request, or letter step lands on you. Previously it only refreshed on a 60-second timer or a manual reload — a step assigned to someone outside the document's own department could take a full minute to appear.

§7 Forms

Structured, fillable templates (leave requests, expense claims, and the like) that produce a real document once submitted, and can be routed through the same approval engine as any other document.

  • Templates — browse the forms available to fill.
  • Fill a Form — pick a template, complete its fields, and submit. A filled form is stored as its own document (with a link back to the template) and follows the normal Draft → In Review → Active lifecycle.
  • My Submissions — every form you've filled, with its current status.

§8 E-Signing

Legally-binding signature ceremonies on a document, separate from (and usually happening after) the approval chain — a document can require approval, a signature, both, or neither depending on its type.

  • New Request — pick a document, add one or more recipients (internal DMS users or external email addresses), and send. Internal recipients see the request appear in My Work and their Pending queue; external recipients get an emailed signing link.
  • Pending My Signature — requests waiting on you. Opening one shows the document and a signature pad / typed-signature option.
  • Sent for Signature — requests you sent, with each recipient's status (pending, signed, declined).
  • Completed — finished ceremonies. The final signed copy is filed back onto the document as a flattened, tamper-evident PDF.

Declining a signature request requires a reason, and immediately notifies whoever sent it. A document already on legal hold can't be signed until the hold is released.

§9 Letters (CAMS)

Correspondence management — incoming and outgoing letters, each with its own reference number, priority, and (for outgoing letters) its own review/approve/dispatch chain, independent of the document approval engine.

  • Incoming letters are registered on receipt and assigned to a person or department to respond to; overdue ones are flagged wherever they appear (My Work, the Letters list).
  • Outgoing letters move through Draft → Review → Approved → Registered → Dispatched, mirroring the document chain in spirit but with its own step assignments.
  • The Chief Executive role is a standing, tenant-wide, read-only recipient for correspondence — an oversight seat, not a participant in any chain.

§10 Bundles & Recycle Bin

Bundles

A bundle groups related documents under one name — a case file, a board pack, a due-diligence set — without moving or copying the underlying documents. Add documents to a bundle from any document's Add to Bundle button, or build one from scratch under Bundles in the sidebar.

Recycle Bin

Deleted documents land in the Recycle Bin before they're gone for good. From there an authorized user can restore a document or, for a records officer or admin, permanently purge it — purging is a deliberate two-person action: one person stages it, a different person (officer or admin) must approve the actual destruction.

§11 Search & AI Assistant

Full-text and metadata search across everything you have read access to, with filters for department, document type, status, and date range. AI Assistant adds document summarization and metadata suggestions — open a document and use Summarize or Suggest metadata from its Overview tab, or ask a question directly from the Assistant page.

§12 Notifications & live updates

The bell icon in the top bar holds every notification you've received — a step assigned to you, a decision on something you submitted, a signature completed, a legal hold released. Most of the app also updates itself live: a second person watching the same document's Workflow tab sees a decision the moment it happens, with no reload needed.

You can turn off specific email notification types (for instance, legal-hold releases) from Settings without affecting what shows up in the bell or on-screen.

§13 Legal hold & retention

Legal hold

Placing a document on legal hold freezes it — no new version, no signature, no disposal — until the hold is explicitly released, regardless of what its retention schedule says. Only Records Officers and admins can apply or release a hold, and every document affected is logged with the reason.

Retention

Every document type carries a retention period counted from its trigger date. When that period lapses, the document is staged for disposal in the Lifecycle Queue; disposal only actually runs after a records officer or admin authorizes it — never automatically — and a document on legal hold is skipped regardless of how overdue it is.

§14 Audit trail & reports

Every meaningful action — upload, checkout, decision, download, hold, disposal — is written to an append-only audit log (the database itself refuses updates or deletes on it, not just the application). A document's own Audit Trail tab shows its history; Reports & Analytics and the Operations console's Audit Logs panel give the tenant-wide view, available to Auditors, Records Officers, Dept Managers, and admins.

§17 Troubleshooting

"I don't see a button/tab I expect"

Check three things in order: (1) your role's permissions — see the reference table; (2) whether an admin has scoped your access to specific folders or departments via an ACL entry; (3) the document's own state — many actions (Check Out, Publish, Discard) only appear for specific statuses.

"Force Unlock / Reassign / Cancel chain says nothing happened"

These are admin-only recovery actions and always require a written reason of a minimum length — if the button looks disabled, type a longer reason first. All three write to the audit log, so there's a record of who released or redirected what, and why.

"I approved something but the other approver still sees it as pending"

This should update within a second or two without a page reload. If it doesn't, check your connection — the live-update channel (a WebSocket) needs a stable connection; a reload always shows the current state regardless.

"A document I need to edit is locked"

Someone else holds its checkout. Ask them to use Cancel Checkout, or, if they're unavailable, ask a Dept Manager or admin to use Force Unlock with a reason.

"I can't sign or move a document"

Check whether it's on legal hold (shown near the top of the document page) — a hold blocks new versions, signing, and disposal until a Records Officer or admin releases it.
